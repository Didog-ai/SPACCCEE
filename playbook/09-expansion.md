## 1. Next product: livestock handling equipment

### Why this one

1. **Same factory, no new machines.** Yard panels, gates, crushes and ramps are welded steel tube and hot-dip galvanised. That's what we already do for trailers.
2. **No road-vehicle compliance.** No ADR, VIN or RAV paperwork. This is the biggest saving compared with any other vehicle-type product.
3. **Packs densely into containers.** Panels and gates stack flat, so they ship cheaply. They also fill the empty space in mixed trailer containers.
4. **We know the buyers already.** Rural merchandise stores, farm machinery dealers and trailer dealers in country towns already sell both trailers and yard gear. That's segment 4 (`rural_ag`) in 02-target-segments.
5. **The market is large and steady.**
   - Australia: about **30.8 million cattle** in 2026 (MLA), with slaughter at its highest level since 1978. About 64-67 million sheep.
   - New Zealand: **3.96 million beef cattle**, up 3.3% in 2026 and rising for several years, plus about 22.8 million sheep (Beef + Lamb NZ).
6. **Our edge is the same as in trailers: steel quality.** Cheap imported panels are often **pre-galvanised tube with paint over the welds**, and farmers complain about them rusting. Australian-made gear is good but expensive. Panels sell for roughly **A$75-170 each**. We can sell **"hot-dip galvanised after fabrication"** at an imported price. That's the gap.
7. **Australian fabricators are under pressure.** Imported fabricated steel is undercutting local fabricators by an average of 36.8% (2026 survey), and fabrication workshops are closing. Buyers will expect to import, and local builders may want a supplier rather than a competitor.

### The range to launch

| Item | Role |
|---|---|
| Heavy-duty cattle panels (2.1 m, 6 rail, oval or 40x80 rail) | Volume line and container filler |
| Sheep and goat panels | Volume line, strongest for NZ |
| Gates in a panel (man gate and vehicle gate) | Sells with every yard |
| Cattle crush with headbail | **Hero product**: high value, where the quality story matters most |
| Loading ramp (fixed and portable) | High value |
| Portable yard kits (for example "20-head starter yard") | Easy to buy: one price, one pallet |

### How to sell it

- **Add it to every rural trailer dealer's next mixed container.** This is the cheapest test, since those dealers are already buying from us or being pitched.
- Pitch rural merchandise chains and independent rural stores, and machinery dealers. Walk the field days in 05-trade-shows-and-events.
- Offer white-label supply to Australian and NZ yard builders who are short of welders.
- Lead every pitch with a cut-open weld sample: **hot-dip galvanised inside and out** compared with paint on the weld.

## 2. Country to focus on: New Zealand

### Why New Zealand

1. **The India-NZ free trade agreement came into force on 20 October 2026.** Indian goods enter New Zealand duty-free.
2. **NZ is a trailer country.** About **800,000 trailers and caravans** are registered, for about 5.3 million people: roughly one for every seven people.
3. **Light trailers are easy to bring in.** NZTA exempts class TA and TB trailers (3,500 kg and under) from entry certification, and light trailers don't need a VIN. Confirm this with an NZ entry certifier before the first shipment.
4. **The livestock range fits perfectly.** About 23 million sheep and a beef herd growing every year.
5. **It's close and similar.** The same buyer culture, the same sea route region, and the owner can visit on the same trips as Australia.

### Countries considered and not chosen

| Country | Why not now |
|---|---|
| **Australia** | Not "not chosen": it stays the core. There is still plenty of room in NSW, WA and SA (01-strategy). The livestock range goes here first. |
| **USA** | The largest market, but in August 2026 the US proposed adding trailers (HS 8716) to the Section 232 steel derivative tariffs at a proposed 15-25% on the **full value**, on top of other tariffs. Too uncertain. Re-check in 2027. |
| **UK** | The India-UK CETA came into force on 15 July 2026 with duty-free access for most goods. But UK trailers need type approval and the trailer culture is smaller. A possible later market for livestock gear only. |
| **EU** | CBAM carbon costs extend to downstream steel products from 2028, and type approval is heavier. Not a fit for a one-person sales team. |

## Caravan chassis: where to take it

Researched November 2026. Australia is the anchor. New Zealand is next. After New Zealand, in priority order:

| # | Country | Duty on Indian trailers/chassis | Market | Main obstacle | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Australia** | Free (ECTA) | 18,438 caravans built locally in 2025 (up 7.3%), each needing a chassis. Local builders are squeezed by Chinese caravan imports and need cheaper chassis. | Chassis must meet ADRs and each builder's drawings | **Start here.** We already hold ADR drawbar and coupling approvals. |
| 1 | **New Zealand** | Free (FTA in force 20 Oct 2026) | Small caravan-building base. Better for trailers. | Small volume | Sell chassis to the few NZ builders on the same trips as trailers. |
| 2 | **United Kingdom** | **0%** on HS 8716 from India (CETA in force 15 July 2026) | 98% of UK touring caravans are built in the UK by a small group of large makers. There is also a large holiday-home (static caravan) sector. | Touring chassis are dominated by lightweight European chassis brands, and touring sales are falling. Road trailers need GB type approval. | **The best market after NZ, with zero duty.** Lead with **holiday-home (static) chassis**, which aren't towed on the road, and off-road campers. Don't chase touring caravan chassis first. |
| 3 | **Canada** | No FTA yet. India-Canada CEPA talks aim to finish by end-2026. Non-FTA countries face steel quotas with a 50% surtax over quota, plus a 25% surtax on listed steel derivative goods. | Small RV-building base, mostly supplied from the US | Tariff uncertainty and Transport Canada manufacturer rules | **Wait for CEPA.** If signed, move it up. Check whether HS 8716 is on Canada's derivative surtax list. |
| 4 | **USA** | 10% now. USTR proposed up to 25% (17% with a trade deal) on 25 Nov 2026. Section 232 proposal adds trailers at 15-25% of full value. | The biggest RV market in the world, but 2026 shipments are forecast at about 290,000, down sharply, with towables falling fastest. | Tariffs stack up, the market is in a downturn, and the big chassis supplier uses US steel and is part of the US RV supply chain | **Watch list.** Re-check once the India-US deal is signed and the Section 232 trailer decision is final. |

**Not on the list:** EU (CBAM from 2028 and type approval; the big German caravan makers are tied to established chassis suppliers). South Africa (strong off-road caravan culture with about 72 local builders, but the camping-trailer market is only about US$71 million).

### Why this order

1. **Duty first.** A chassis is mostly steel and labour. A 15-25% tariff on full value wipes out our price advantage. Australia, NZ and the UK are duty-free; Canada and the US are not.
2. **Then compliance we already have.** ADR approvals transfer straight to Australian builders. The UK is the first market that needs new approvals, and static chassis avoid most of that.
3. **Then the number of buyers we can reach.** Australia has many mid-size builders. The UK has a few large ones. That's slower to win but each win is big.
4. **The US is the biggest prize but the worst timing.** Keep it as an option, not a plan.

### Triggers to change the order

- India-Canada CEPA signed with steel derivatives duty-free → move Canada to #2.
- India-US deal signed **and** trailers left out of Section 232 → put the US ahead of Canada.
- UK holiday-home chassis trial order won → double down on the UK before Canada.

### UK: chassis or trailers? (owner asked, November 2026)

**Holiday-home (static caravan and lodge) chassis are the better UK operation.** Don't go into UK road trailers.

| | Holiday-home chassis | Road trailers |
|---|---|---|
| Road approval | None: units are moved on low-loaders and built to BS 3632 / NCC Code of Practice 501 | GB type approval needed; our ADR/CTA approvals don't count |
| Buyers | About a dozen factories, most clustered around Hull, East Yorkshire (Willerby, Swift, ABI, Victory, Carnaby, Delta and others) | Many small dealers and farm stores, a strong local market leader, and buyers who expect a UK parts and warranty network |
| Competition | One dominant supplier (AL-KO, formerly Bankside Patterson), so the factories want a second source | Established UK brands plus imports from Belgium, Poland, Germany and China |
| Our edge | Fully galvanised chassis are the spec for coastal parks. That's our strength. | Price only |
| Value per sale | One large, high-value chassis per home, in factory volumes | Low value per unit, sold one at a time |

**Risks to solve before quoting:**
- Length: chassis are often longer than a 40 ft container (about 12 m inside). Ship as a kit of main beams and cross-members, assembled in the UK, or use flat racks.
- Just-in-time supply: the factories expect weekly delivery. Hold buffer stock in a Hull-area warehouse.
- Standards: confirm with the NCC and one manufacturer what BS 3632 / CoP 501 testing a new chassis supplier needs.
- Volume data: the latest UK holiday-home dispatch figures we found are from 2017, at about 21,000-22,000 units a year. Get 2025 figures from the NCC.

**First move:** visit 3-4 of the smaller Hull-area builders and offer a trial batch of fully galvanised lodge or pod chassis as a second source.
