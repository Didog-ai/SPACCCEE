# Business Model: The Lean Way to Scale

Written November 2026. The question: what's the leanest and fastest way to scale a factory that can make almost any steel product, across AUS, NZ, UK, Canada and the US?

## The short answer

**Don't own warehouses or carry dealer stock. Sell full containers straight from the factory, get paid when the container ships, and let one stocking partner per country hold the stock.** Scale by adding contract customers (caravan builders, holiday-home factories, big dealers) and Indian partner factories, not buildings.

## Why "a big warehouse in every country" doesn't scale

The idea gets one thing right (dealers ordering whole containers, cheaper per unit the more they order) and one thing wrong (owning the stock).

The cost that kills it isn't warehouse rent. It's **cash tied up in stock**. Illustrative maths, assuming about **A$80k landed value per container** (TO CONFIRM with real prices):

| | Warehouse in every country + pay-when-sold | Factory-direct, paid at shipment |
|---|---|---|
| Production and sea freight (we've paid for steel and labour) | ~3 months | ~1.5 months, part-covered by a 30% deposit |
| Sitting in our warehouse | ~2 months | 0 |
| Waiting for the dealer to sell and pay | ~3 months | 0 (paid against the bill of lading or a letter of credit) |
| **Months of sales tied up** | **~8** | **~1** |
| Cash tied up at 10 containers/month | **~A$6.4m** | **~A$0.8m** |
| Cash tied up at 50 containers/month (5 countries) | **~A$32m** | **~A$4m** |
| Fixed costs | 5 leases, staff, forklifts, insurance, stocktake losses | Almost none |
| Risk if a country flops | Stuck with stock and a lease | Stop shipping |

Owning warehouses also makes us the retailer's warehouse, which competes with the dealers we sell to, and it forces us to guess demand in five countries at once.

## The lean model: four parts

### 1. Factory-direct containers ("order, build, ship")

- Customers order **whole containers** (mixed loads allowed) from a fixed catalogue.
- **Terms:** 30% deposit with the order, balance against the bill of lading (when it ships) or a letter of credit. We never carry stock after it leaves the port.
- **Volume pricing** per order: 1, 3, 5 and 10+ containers, plus an **annual rebate** for hitting a yearly volume. This rewards commitment without us holding stock.
- **Rolling forecast:** big customers give a 3-month forecast, with month 1 firm. The factory plans steel buying and production from it.

**Delivery terms: the buyer collects at the port.**
- Default price is **CIF to the buyer's nearest main port** (for example Brisbane, Melbourne, Sydney, Fremantle, Adelaide, Auckland or Hull). We book and pay the sea freight and cargo insurance. Risk passes to the buyer when the container is loaded in India.
- The buyer's customs broker clears it and pays the duty (usually 0% under the trade deals), import GST/VAT and port charges. The buyer trucks it to their yard, unpacks it and returns the empty container within the shipping line's free days.
- **We keep control until paid:** the shipping line releases the container only when we release the bill of lading (or a telex release). We do that only when the balance is paid.
- For first-time importers, offer **delivered to their yard (DAP)** as an add-on through our freight forwarder, at cost plus a margin. The buyer still clears customs and pays GST/VAT.
- **Avoid DDP** (delivered with duty paid). It makes us the importer of record in each country, with the tax registration and legal liability that brings.
- Every container must meet the destination's biosecurity rules: clean steel, ISPM-15 treated timber, and a clean container. Australia and NZ inspect strictly.

### 2. One stocking partner per country (the warehouse we don't own)

Small dealers can't take a whole container. Instead of our own warehouse:
- Appoint one **master distributor** per country, or per region in Australia. A strong existing dealer or wholesaler is ideal.
- They buy containers at the best tier price, hold stock, and sell to small dealers at a margin.
- They own the stock, the lease and the debt. We give them the range, marketing material and a regional territory **in return for minimum volumes**. Lose the territory if they miss the minimums.
- Only if a country passes about **10 containers/month** and the partner can't keep up: use a **3PL warehouse** (pay per pallet, no lease) near the port. Never a building of our own.

### 3. Contract customers before dealers (the fastest growth)

One caravan builder or UK holiday-home factory can take **several containers a month on contract**, with no stock and no retail selling. One dealer typically takes 1.
- **Build-to-print / white-label:** make chassis, kits and trailers to the customer's drawings under their brand.
- Target: caravan builders (AUS), holiday-home factories around Hull (UK), trailer builders short of welders (AUS/NZ).
- Every contract gets a 12-month supply agreement with volume bands and price review dates.

### 4. Focus the factory: "any steel product" is a capability, not a strategy

Scale comes from repetition, not variety. Every new product brings new drawings, jigs, approvals, spare parts and sales conversations.
- Keep a **core catalogue of 20-30 SKUs** designed to pack densely into a container.
- Use **shared components** across products: the same rails, cross-members, axles, couplings and galvanising across trailers and caravan chassis.
- Do custom work **only above a minimum order** (for example 2 containers) or under contract.
- One new product line a year at most. Caravan chassis now, livestock equipment next.

## Credit: replace pay-when-sold

Pay-when-sold makes us the bank, and the bank is what runs out first.
- **Keep it only for the first trial container** with a new dealer, capped and registered on the PPSR (07-credit-and-risk).
- From the second order: deposit + balance at shipment, a letter of credit, or **credit insured by someone else**: Indian export credit insurance (ECGC) or a trade-credit insurer, with an invoice-finance facility so we're paid in days while the customer gets 60-90 days.
- Offer a small **prepayment discount** (say 2-3%). It's cheaper than funding the stock.

## People: don't be the bottleneck

The owner is the only salesperson. That caps growth more than factory capacity does.
- **Country agents on commission** (paid per container shipped), not employees, to find and service customers. One per country to start.
- The master distributor handles small dealers. The owner only handles the master distributor and contract customers.
- The bot does prospect research, outreach drafts, follow-ups and reporting.

## Capacity: when we pass 20 containers/month

- **Partner fabricators in India**, building to our drawings under our quality checks (our inspectors, our weld procedures, our galvanising spec). That's capacity without capital.
- Build or extend our own factory only when partner volumes are steady for 6+ months.

## Rollout: one country at a time, with gates

| Phase | Countries | Gate to move on |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Fix the model at home | AUS (and NZ via the same team) | New terms in place; a master distributor in at least 2 regions; 1 caravan-builder contract; 10 containers/month |
| 2. First new country | UK (holiday-home chassis contract first) | 1 UK factory contract shipping monthly; cash cycle under 2 months |
| 3. Next | Canada once the India-Canada deal is signed; the US once tariffs are settled | The trigger events in 09-expansion |

**Every country entry follows the same playbook:** 1 contract customer or 1 master distributor first, a country agent second, a 3PL only if volume proves it.

## The numbers to manage the business by

- **Cash conversion cycle** (days from paying for steel to receiving payment). Target under 60 days.
- **% of revenue paid at or before shipment.** Target 80%+.
- **Containers per customer per month**, and the share of volume under contract.
- **Forecast accuracy** of the rolling 3-month order book.
- **Factory utilisation** (containers made ÷ capacity).
